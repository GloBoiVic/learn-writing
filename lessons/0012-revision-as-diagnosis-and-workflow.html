III. Shape thought

Revision as Diagnosis and Workflow

Revision is not a final polish pass. It is diagnosis: identify what the reader needs, locate the failure, change the level that caused it, and then verify the result in a separate pass.

Read for understandingPractice and testing come later
Why this matters. Your goal is to revise without outsourcing judgment to a tool or a vague feeling that the prose is “better.” A repeatable workflow lets you decide what kind of change a draft needs.
By the end of this lesson
  • Distinguish developmental, structural, paragraph, sentence, copyediting, and proofreading work.
  • Use a reverse outline to find missing or competing paragraph jobs.
  • Run separate passes for purpose, evidence, information flow, sentence control, and consistency.
The map
purpose→structure→evidence→paragraph→sentence→copy→proof
Core ideas

Revise at the right level

A comma cannot repair a missing argument. A stronger verb cannot repair an answer that does not address the prompt. Start at the highest level where the problem appears and move downward.

Reverse outline

After drafting, write the actual job of each paragraph in the margin. Compare that sequence with the reader’s questions. A paragraph with two jobs may need to split; a paragraph with no distinct job may need to move or disappear.

Separate passes

Each pass should ask one kind of question: purpose, structure, evidence, links, sentence emphasis, copy, then proof. Separating passes reduces the temptation to polish a sentence while ignoring a larger problem.

Worked choices
Diagnose before trimming
Less controlled

The paragraph explains the need, describes the organization’s history, lists three staff roles, and introduces the evaluation plan.

More deliberate

Need paragraph -> why the condition matters.
Capacity paragraph -> why this organization can respond.
Evaluation paragraph -> how change will be observed.

If a paragraph feels wordy because it contains competing jobs, cutting adjectives will not solve the problem. Reorganize first, then edit sentences.

A revision log
Less controlled

Use “clients” in one field, “residents” in another, and “participants” in a third without explanation.

More deliberate

Choose the term that fits the population being described, record the decision, and check every occurrence across the application.

A small decision log protects consistency and prevents later polishing from introducing contradictions.

Use this decision rule

Revision pass order

  • Purpose: What must this reader understand, decide, or do?
  • Structure: Does the order answer the reader’s questions?
  • Evidence: Are claims supported, interpreted, and qualified?
  • Paragraphs: Does each paragraph have one job and a handoff?
  • Sentences: Are actors, actions, relationships, and emphasis visible?
  • Copy and proof: Are terms, numbers, citations, and formatting consistent?
Watch for these traps
  • Editing from the first sentence to the last without changing levels.
  • Treating a thesaurus pass as revision.
  • Letting an AI rewrite conceal the fact that the argument is still incomplete.
Carry forward. The practice phase will give you drafts to revise. For now, learn the order of questions so that later practice strengthens judgment instead of creating random preferences.
