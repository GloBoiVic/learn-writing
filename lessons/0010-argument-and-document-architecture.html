III. Shape thought

Argument and Document Architecture

A document is an argument at several scales. The purpose determines the sections; each section answers a reader question; each paragraph develops one claim; sentences make the relationships visible.

Read for understandingPractice and testing come later
Why this matters. A grant application is not a collection of isolated answers. A professional document earns trust when its parts reinforce one another without repeating the same information mechanically.
By the end of this lesson
  • Distinguish claim, evidence, interpretation, and qualification.
  • Order sections according to the reader’s decision path.
  • Use headings and paragraph openings to orient a busy reader.
The map
reader question→claim→evidence→interpretation→next question
Core ideas

Claims need interpretation

Evidence does not speak for itself. A claim tells the reader what to believe or understand; evidence supports it; interpretation explains why the evidence matters; qualification keeps the claim within what the evidence can support.

Architecture follows decisions

Order sections by the questions the reader must answer. A proposal may move from need to response to capacity to evidence to measures to budget, but the exact order should follow the funder’s instructions and decision path.

Headings are promises

A useful heading tells the reader what information follows. A paragraph opening should orient the reader to the claim, not merely repeat the section label.

Worked choices
Evidence with interpretation
Less controlled

211 recorded 15,087 Waukesha contacts.

More deliberate

211 recorded 15,087 Waukesha contacts in the reporting period. That volume indicates sustained demand for navigation and supports testing an additional, accessible entry point.

The second version does not pretend the number proves every part of the proposal. It states the reasonable inference and keeps the proposed response proportionate.

Section order
Less controlled

Organization history -> board biography -> need -> proposed project -> measures

More deliberate

Defined need -> proposed response -> capacity to deliver -> expected change -> measures and budget

The second order follows the reviewer’s decision path. Organizational facts can appear where they establish relevance or capacity instead of delaying the case.

Use this decision rule

Build the argument

  • What question is the reader trying to answer?
  • What is your direct answer?
  • What evidence supports it?
  • What does the evidence allow you to infer?
  • What limitation or uncertainty should a credible reader see?
Watch for these traps
  • Confusing more data with a stronger case.
  • Leading with institutional biography because it is easy to reuse.
  • Repeating the same claim in every field instead of giving each field a distinct job.
Carry forward. Structure is not a cosmetic outline added after drafting. It is the visible shape of the reasoning the reader must perform.
