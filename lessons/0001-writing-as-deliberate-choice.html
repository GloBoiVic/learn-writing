I. Build the map

Writing as Deliberate Choice

Writing is a chain of decisions: situation, purpose, reader, structure, sentence, word, and revision. Good prose makes the intended relationship among those decisions easy to see.

Read for understandingPractice and testing come later
Why this matters. Your goal is not to sound impressive. It is to help a particular reader understand something accurately and then know what to think, decide, or do.
By the end of this lesson
  • Map a writing problem from situation to revision instead of starting with isolated wording.
  • Separate grammatical correctness from rhetorical fitness.
  • Treat voice as a pattern of choices that can adapt without becoming generic.
The map
situation→purpose→reader→structure→language→revision
Core ideas

The rhetorical situation

Every piece of writing has a situation: a writer with knowledge and limits, a reader with questions and constraints, a purpose, a medium, and stakes. A grant response, a maintenance email, and a personal reflection may describe the same event but require different choices because their situations differ.

Grammar and rhetoric

Grammar gives you available structures. Rhetoric is the judgment of choosing among them for this reader and purpose. A short sentence is not automatically clear, and a long sentence is not automatically bad.

The working sequence

Start by naming what the reader needs to understand or decide. Then choose an order that answers that need, shape the paragraphs and sentences, choose precise words, and revise against the reader task.

Worked choices
A grant opening
Less controlled

IMPACT is committed to leveraging innovative solutions to increase access for vulnerable populations.

More deliberate

Funding would place 20 information kiosks in community sites where residents already seek help, giving them another way to find verified local services.

The second sentence names the work, location, population, and practical result. It is not merely shorter; its structure lets a reviewer see the case.

A practical email
Less controlled

I wanted to reach out regarding the ongoing situation with the maintenance issue.

More deliberate

I am following up because no maintenance staff has visited, and the leak is still active.

The direct version preserves a calm tone while putting the situation and consequence where the reader can act on them.

Use this decision rule

Before drafting

  • What must this reader understand, decide, or do?
  • What evidence or context will let the reader trust the answer?
  • What order will make the answer easiest to recover?
  • What wording will sound like a responsible person in this situation?
Watch for these traps
  • Starting with a polished sentence before naming the point.
  • Treating institutional language as a model of good prose.
  • Confusing brevity with clarity.
Carry forward. Use this map whenever a draft feels wrong. The problem may not be a word problem. It may be an unclear purpose, missing evidence, or an order that asks the reader to reconstruct the argument.
