# Deliberate Prose: Course Map

*16 lessons that provide the information needed before practice: writing judgment, sentence control, prose structure, voice, and grant application credibility.*

**This phase teaches** a complete map of the choices you will later practice: what to notice, what it means, and how to decide.

**This phase does not test.** No quizzes, gates, or required retrieval yet. The practice phase begins when you say you are ready.

## I. Build the map

See the whole writing system and learn the sentence relationships that make thought visible.

1. [Writing as Deliberate Choice](lessons/0001-writing-as-deliberate-choice.md): Writing is a chain of decisions: situation, purpose, reader, structure, sentence, word, and revision. Good prose makes the intended relationship among those decisions easy to see.
2. [The Sentence Map: Core, Roles, and Parts](lessons/0002-the-sentence-map-core-roles-and-parts.md): A sentence becomes easier to revise when you can see its clause core: subject, finite verb, and whatever completes the verb. Modifiers add detail, but they should not hide the main action.
3. [Relationships and Boundaries](lessons/0003-relationships-and-boundaries.md): Clauses let you show whether ideas are equal, sequential, causal, conditional, contrasting, or concessive. Punctuation and conjunctions should reveal that relationship instead of leaving the reader to guess.

## II. Control sentences

Make sentences reliable, readable, specific, and deliberately paced.

4. [Sentence Reliability: Attachment, Agreement, Pronouns, and Voice](lessons/0004-sentence-reliability-attachment-agreement-pronouns-and-voice.md): Reliable sentences make responsibility, reference, time, and agency recoverable. Modifiers should attach clearly, subjects and verbs should agree, pronouns should point somewhere definite, and voice should foreground the right participant.
5. [Parallelism, Word Order, and Emphasis](lessons/0005-parallelism-word-order-and-emphasis.md): Readers compare parallel forms quickly. They also give extra attention to the beginning and ending of a sentence. Use form and position to show what belongs together and what deserves emphasis.
6. [Punctuation as Structural Control](lessons/0006-punctuation-as-structural-control.md): Punctuation marks relationships and boundaries. It is not primarily a record of where the writer pauses to breathe. Find the structure first, then choose the mark that supports the intended reading.
7. [Clarity and Information Flow](lessons/0007-clarity-and-information-flow.md): Clarity is partly an information-order problem. Readers need a recognizable starting point, a visible relationship, and a useful arrival. Put the actor or topic where it can orient the reader, then move toward the new point.
8. [Concrete Prose: Verbs, Nominalizations, Precision, and Jargon](lessons/0008-concrete-prose-verbs-nominalizations-precision-and-jargon.md): Concrete prose makes claims that readers can picture, check, and use. Strong verbs help, but the larger goal is to expose actors, actions, quantities, and limits instead of hiding them inside abstractions or specialized language.

## III. Shape thought

Build paragraphs and arguments, then carry a natural voice into professional writing.

9. [Cadence, Cohesion, and Paragraphs](lessons/0009-cadence-cohesion-and-paragraphs.md): Paragraphs are units of thought, not containers with a required number of sentences. Cohesion links the sentences; cadence gives the prose felt movement. Both should serve the reader’s understanding rather than perform style.
10. [Argument and Document Architecture](lessons/0010-argument-and-document-architecture.md): A document is an argument at several scales. The purpose determines the sections; each section answers a reader question; each paragraph develops one claim; sentences make the relationships visible.
11. [Voice, Tone, Register, and Professional Writing](lessons/0011-voice-tone-register-and-professional-writing.md): Voice is the recurring pattern of choices a writer makes: specificity, syntax, pace, diction, stance, qualification, and relationship to the reader. Tone changes with the situation; register changes with the discourse community. Voice is not a costume or a collection of quirks.
12. [Revision as Diagnosis and Workflow](lessons/0012-revision-as-diagnosis-and-workflow.md): Revision is not a final polish pass. It is diagnosis: identify what the reader needs, locate the failure, change the level that caused it, and then verify the result in a separate pass.

## IV. Write for funders

Translate the system into prompt reading, grant architecture, evidence, measures, and credibility.

13. [Reading Funder Questions and Building Application Architecture](lessons/0013-reading-funder-questions-and-building-application-architecture.md): A grant prompt is both a specification and a reviewer’s question. Before drafting, extract its required elements, verbs, limits, timeframe, and evidence standard, then map each answer into one coherent application argument.
14. [Need, Population, Evidence, and Ethical Specificity](lessons/0014-need-population-evidence-and-ethical-specificity.md): A needs statement names a condition, who experiences it, the local context, and the evidence that supports the claim. It distinguishes the underlying need from the requested service and describes people with accuracy and respect.
15. [Project Design, Capacity, Collaboration, and Sustainability](lessons/0015-project-design-capacity-collaboration-and-sustainability.md): A project becomes credible when the reader can trace resources to activities, outputs, and intended outcomes, and can see who is responsible for each part. Capacity, collaboration, equity, and sustainability need operational evidence rather than labels.
16. [Outcomes, Measures, Budgets, and Whole-Application Credibility](lessons/0016-outcomes-measures-budgets-and-whole-application-credibility.md): The final grant-writing judgment is coherence: the need, project, budget, measures, and claims must describe the same work at the same scale. Credible voice emerges when the application is specific, proportionate, and internally consistent.
