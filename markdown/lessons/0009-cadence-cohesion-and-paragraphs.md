# Lesson 09: Cadence, Cohesion, and Paragraphs

*III. Shape thought · Deliberate Prose, knowledge phase. Read for understanding; practice and testing come later.*

**Paragraphs are units of thought, not containers with a required number of sentences. Cohesion links the sentences; cadence gives the prose felt movement. Both should serve the reader’s understanding rather than perform style.**

> **Why this matters.** Your natural writing already has tendencies in pace and sequencing. This lesson gives you language for keeping the useful tendencies while shaping a paragraph into a clear handoff.

## By the end of this lesson

- Give each paragraph one controlling job.
- Link sentences through repeated terms, clear references, sequence, and actual relationships.
- Vary sentence length and openings for function rather than decoration.

## The map

**controlling idea → development → evidence → interpretation → handoff**

## Core ideas

### The paragraph job

A paragraph may orient, explain, prove, compare, qualify, or recommend. Its sentences should cooperate toward that job. A paragraph that feels wordy may contain two competing ideas rather than too many words.

### Cohesion

Cohesion comes from repeated key terms, clear pronouns, parallel structures, logical sequence, and meaningful transitions. A transition word cannot create a relationship that the ideas do not have.

### Cadence

Cadence is the movement created by syntax, stress, sound, and sentence length. A short sentence can land a consequence. A longer sentence can hold conditions and evidence. Variation should make the thought easier to feel and follow.

## Worked choices

### From list to paragraph

**Less controlled**

> The pilot uses community sites. The sites host kiosks. Staff update the directory. Residents can find services. The program tracks usage.

**More deliberate**

> The pilot extends access through community sites that host information kiosks. Staff update the directory so residents can find verified services, and the program tracks usage to show whether the additional entry point is being used.

*Why:* The revision gives the paragraph one job: explain how the pilot extends access. The repeated topic becomes a chain instead of five disconnected starts.

### A useful landing

**Less controlled**

> The team met, reviewed the data, discussed several options, and made plans for the next quarter.

**More deliberate**

> After reviewing the data, the team met to decide what the next quarter required. The decision depended on one question: which barrier should the pilot address first?

*Why:* The short second sentence creates a landing and points the next paragraph toward a decision.

## Use this decision rule: Paragraph inspection

- Write the paragraph’s actual job in the margin.
- Identify the controlling idea and the evidence that develops it.
- Check each pronoun and repeated term for a clear link.
- Remove transitions that merely announce movement without naming a relationship.
- Read the ending: does it resolve, qualify, or hand off the thought?

## Watch for these traps

- Making every paragraph the same length.
- Adding “Additionally” or “However” instead of fixing the logic.
- Varying sentence length so aggressively that the paragraph loses its topic.

> **Carry forward.** A reader should be able to say what a paragraph is doing and why its next sentence belongs. That is cohesion at the scale where most professional prose succeeds or fails.
