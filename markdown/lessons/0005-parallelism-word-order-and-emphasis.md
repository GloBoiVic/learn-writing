# Lesson 05: Parallelism, Word Order, and Emphasis

*II. Control sentences · Deliberate Prose, knowledge phase. Read for understanding; practice and testing come later.*

**Readers compare parallel forms quickly. They also give extra attention to the beginning and ending of a sentence. Use form and position to show what belongs together and what deserves emphasis.**

> **Why this matters.** Lists of activities, outcomes, and responsibilities are common in grant applications. Parallel structure makes those lists usable instead of forcing the reviewer to re-parse each item.

## By the end of this lesson

- Make comparable items grammatically comparable.
- Place familiar information before new information when that improves the reader’s path.
- Use sentence openings and endings deliberately rather than decorating every sentence.

## The map

**known topic → development → new point**

## Core ideas

### Parallel forms

Units that a reader should compare should look and behave alike: “install kiosks, train hosts, and collect data.” Parallelism is both a correctness aid and a way to show the structure of a plan.

### Known and new information

A sentence often works best when it begins from something the reader already knows and ends with the point the reader needs to absorb. This is a tendency, not a rigid rule.

### Emphasis positions

Beginnings orient the reader; endings land with force. Put the topic early and the consequence, qualification, or key term late when that arrangement serves the purpose.

## Worked choices

### A project list

**Less controlled**

> The pilot will install kiosks, train hosts, and data will be collected.

**More deliberate**

> The pilot will install kiosks, train hosts, and collect data.

*Why:* The corrected list gives all three activities the same grammatical form. The reader can now compare them immediately.

### A sentence ending

**Less controlled**

> The program used a new referral process to improve access for residents.

**More deliberate**

> For residents who cannot reach an office during business hours, the new referral process provides another path to verified services.

*Why:* The second version establishes the affected residents first and ends on the practical result. The first may be preferable when the program, not the residents, is the topic.

## Use this decision rule: Shape the comparison

- Which units should the reader compare?
- Do those units use matching grammatical forms?
- What is already available in the reader’s context?
- What word or consequence deserves the final position?

## Watch for these traps

- Making every sentence end with a dramatic word.
- Treating parallelism as a demand for repetitive triplets.
- Moving the subject away from the topic simply to vary sentence openings.

> **Carry forward.** Use parallelism to expose the shape of a plan. Use word order to control the reader’s starting point and landing point. Both are tools for thought, not ornament.
