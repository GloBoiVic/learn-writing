# Lesson 07: Clarity and Information Flow

*II. Control sentences · Deliberate Prose, knowledge phase. Read for understanding; practice and testing come later.*

**Clarity is partly an information-order problem. Readers need a recognizable starting point, a visible relationship, and a useful arrival. Put the actor or topic where it can orient the reader, then move toward the new point.**

> **Why this matters.** Funders read quickly and comparatively. A clear sentence reduces recovery work so the reviewer can judge the need, response, and evidence instead of decoding the prose.

## By the end of this lesson

- Recognize when a sentence hides its actor or topic.
- Order familiar and new information for a smoother path.
- Revise dense prose without reducing an important qualification or limit.

## The map

**reader knows → sentence develops → reader learns**

## Core ideas

### The subject as a guide

A meaningful grammatical subject gives the reader a topic to follow. When possible, make an actor, affected population, or concrete thing carry the sentence instead of an abstract process.

### Old to new

Readers often understand a sentence more easily when it begins with information already active in the context and ends with what is newly important. Paragraphs can establish the old information before the sentence arrives at the new point.

### Clarity is not simplification

Plain language can preserve technical accuracy and necessary nuance. The goal is not to remove complexity but to make its structure visible.

## Worked choices

### Actor and action

**Less controlled**

> The improvement of access was achieved through the implementation of kiosks.

**More deliberate**

> The kiosks improved access.

*Why:* The revision gives the sentence a concrete actor and an active verb. Add the population, location, or evidence in the next sentence if the claim needs qualification.

### Information flow

**Less controlled**

> A new way for residents to find services is what the pilot provides through community sites.

**More deliberate**

> The pilot gives residents another way to find services at community sites.

*Why:* The second version starts with the program as the active topic and ends with the practical arrangement. It is easier to extend into evidence or a next sentence.

## Use this decision rule: Information-flow check

- What is the reader already thinking about?
- What should be the sentence topic?
- What is the new point or consequence?
- Can the reader identify the actor and relationship on the first pass?

## Watch for these traps

- Replacing every noun with a verb even when the concept itself is the topic.
- Removing qualifiers that protect accuracy.
- Calling a sentence clear merely because each individual word is familiar.

> **Carry forward.** When prose feels stiff, first test the order of information and the choice of subject. Word-level trimming comes after the reader’s path is sound.
