# Lesson 04: Sentence Reliability: Attachment, Agreement, Pronouns, and Voice

*II. Control sentences · Deliberate Prose, knowledge phase. Read for understanding; practice and testing come later.*

**Reliable sentences make responsibility, reference, time, and agency recoverable. Modifiers should attach clearly, subjects and verbs should agree, pronouns should point somewhere definite, and voice should foreground the right participant.**

> **Why this matters.** In professional and grant writing, a small grammatical ambiguity can misstate who acted, who benefited, or what the organization actually knows. Reliability is part of credibility.

## By the end of this lesson

- Repair a misplaced modifier and an ambiguous pronoun.
- Use tense and aspect to place work accurately in time.
- Choose active or passive voice based on topic and responsibility, not fashion.

## The map

**actor → action → time → referent → topic**

## Core ideas

### Attachment

An introductory modifier should logically describe the grammatical subject that follows it. “After reviewing the data, the report was revised” implies that the report reviewed the data.

### Reference and agreement

A pronoun saves space only when its antecedent is clear and its number and person fit. Subject-verb agreement is usually simple, but long phrases between subject and verb can conceal the real subject.

### Voice and time

Active voice foregrounds an actor. Passive voice foregrounds the acted-on thing or omits an unknown actor. Tense locates an action; aspect presents it as ongoing, completed, or prior. Use the form that tells the truth about responsibility and sequence.

## Worked choices

### Modifier and responsibility

**Less controlled**

> After reviewing the data, the report was revised by staff, and it clarified the need.

**More deliberate**

> After reviewing the data, staff revised the report. The revised report clarified the need.

*Why:* The revision gives the reviewer an accountable actor and removes the vague pronoun “it.” Two sentences are justified because the actor and result deserve separate focus.

### Active and passive voice

**Less controlled**

> Five hundred records were verified during the pilot.

**More deliberate**

> Staff verified 500 records during the pilot.

*Why:* The active version is stronger when staff performance matters. The passive version remains appropriate when the records are the topic or the actor is unknown or irrelevant.

## Use this decision rule: Reliability check

- Who or what performed each important action?
- Does every modifier attach to the intended noun?
- Can every pronoun be traced without rereading?
- Is the tense accurate, and does the aspect show the action as ongoing or complete when needed?
- Should the reader notice the actor, the affected thing, or the process?

## Watch for these traps

- Treating passive voice as automatically weak.
- Adding a pronoun to avoid repeating a noun when the reference becomes unclear.
- Assuming a comma can fix a modifier whose subject is wrong.

> **Carry forward.** Before polishing a sentence, verify its factual relationships. A smooth sentence that assigns the wrong actor or time is still a failed sentence.
