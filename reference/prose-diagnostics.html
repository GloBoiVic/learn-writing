Prose Diagnostics

Start with the likely cause, not the surface symptom.

SymptomLikely causeFirst diagnostic move
Stiff or inflatedAbstract nouns, weak verbs, jargon, or borrowed institutional languageCircle nominalizations; restore actors and verbs; replace claims with particulars.
Hard to followUnclear information order or hidden relationshipsUnderline old information and new information; map the clause cores.
MonotonousRepeated openings, lengths, or syntactic shapesMap sentence openings and clause shapes; vary by function.
WordyOften a structure problem, not extra wordsWrite each paragraph's actual job; split competing ideas before trimming.
Weak endingThe point is buried before the final positionMove the consequence, qualification, or key term to the ending.
Generic voiceUnowned abstractions, inflated verbs, and no concrete particularsReplace uplift with an observable action, person, place, number, or limit.
Untrustworthy grant claimScope, numbers, populations, or causal claims do not alignTrace the claim horizontally across the application and record its evidence.
