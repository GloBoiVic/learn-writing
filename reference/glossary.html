Deliberate Prose / Reference

Glossary

Terms are useful only when they help you see and revise a real choice.

TermWorking definition
clauseA group of words with a subject and finite verb; an independent clause can stand alone.
predicateEverything said about the subject, including its verb and complements.
complementA word or phrase that completes a verb's meaning, such as an object or subject complement.
modifierA word, phrase, or clause that limits, describes, or otherwise specifies another element.
coordinationJoining units as grammatical peers, often with and, but, or, or a semicolon.
subordinationMaking one clause depend on another to express time, cause, condition, contrast, or concession.
syntaxThe arrangement of words, phrases, and clauses in a sentence.
cadenceThe felt movement of prose created by syntax, stress, sound, and sentence shape.
nominalizationA noun formed from a verb or adjective, such as implementation from implement.
parallelismUsing matching grammatical forms for units readers should compare.
rhetorical situationThe writer, reader, purpose, medium, constraints, and stakes surrounding a piece of writing.
rhetoricThe deliberate use of language and structure for a reader, purpose, and situation.
cohesionThe links that make sentences and paragraphs feel connected.
voiceThe recurring pattern of choices in a writer's syntax, vocabulary, pace, stance, specificity, and qualification.
toneThe writer's stance toward a particular reader and situation.
registerThe expected level of formality and specialized vocabulary in a context.
antecedentThe noun or noun phrase a pronoun refers to.
aspectThe verb form that presents an action as ongoing, completed, or prior.
claimA proposition the writer asks the reader to accept or act on.
evidenceInformation that supports a claim when its relevance and limits are explained.
outputA direct product of activities: sessions held, records verified, or people served.
outcomeA change expected to result from the work: knowledge, behavior, condition, or system performance.
indicatorAn observable sign used as evidence of an output or outcome.
measureA defined method, source, timing, and unit for observing an indicator.
